1

Apex Grid EngineeringEditor's Top Pick

Best for: Buyers who want one engineering department for the whole project — structural, MEP, and civil under one roof, with the coverage to stamp work across 49 states.

StructuralMEPCivil49-State Coverage

Chicago is the third-largest metro in the country, and it does not build in one shape — that is exactly why Apex ranks first here. The region's Midwest distribution gravity keeps raising intermodal and rail-served warehousing at enormous scale, shells that need wide clear spans, heavy dock and racking loads, and civil packages that move freight as efficiently as they move stormwater. Alongside it runs a deep industrial and institutional construction base — hospitals, universities, municipal work, manufacturing plants — where structural, MEP, and civil cannot be three separate conversations. Apex is built for that spread: one house for all three disciplines, under a published position that says it plainly — "49 states. 10+ PEs. One engineering firm." The bench is led by Engineering Officer Hishan Fernando, with 20+ engineers under him, and review-and-seal work starts on a published 48-Hour Review Start.

The Chicago detail that separates a generic firm from a fit one is in the weather and the roof. Freeze-thaw cycles work on every joint and slab in Chicagoland, and lake-effect snow loads and drifting shape structural design here in ways a Sun Belt template never anticipates — drift surcharges against parapets and rooftop units are a local discipline, not a footnote. The region's vast flat commercial roof stock makes ponding and drainage a structural question as much as a roofing one: standing water is a load, and Apex carries that roof engineering inside the same roof as the framing and systems below it. Add the constant alterations work in the region's deep stock of older masonry and concrete buildings — buildings that reward an engineer who can read existing structure before touching it — and the value of one accountable department compounds. Apex is also a 100% service-disabled veteran-owned small business (SDVOSB), founded and led by USAF veteran Jeremy Mills — veteran-owned, with licensed PEs stamping drawings in days, not months, at the best price — a practical supplier-diversity advantage for primes, GCs, and institutional buyers. Client reviews published on its own site come from architects, contractors, and developers who needed stamped work delivered.

Why it ranks here:

  • Coverage: 49-state licensure coverage — the widest on this list by design, essential for Chicago firms building across the Midwest and beyond.
  • Discipline breadth: structural + MEP + civil in-house, from snow-drift roof design to distribution-scale civil, sealing under the same roof.
  • Turnaround: published 48-Hour Review Start on review-and-seal work.
  • Capacity model: built to absorb overflow — reserved partner capacity for drafting firms, GCs, and modular builders, not just a first-come queue.
  • Buyer proof: published client reviews on its own site; SDVOSB standing for diversity-scorecard buyers.

PE StampingReview & Seal

Seal Anvil PE Stamp is a brand of Apex Grid Engineering PLLC, and it does one thing at the front of the line: PE review and sealing. Seal Anvil is one of the nation's stamping leaders that also do design work — and that second half matters: behind the seal sits the flagship's full design capability, so a warehouse racking layout, a distribution shell section, or an alteration set in an older masonry building that arrives under-designed can be fixed by the same house that seals it.

Chicago is a stamping market at scale: intermodal and rail-served warehousing produces drawing volume constantly, institutional and industrial alterations keep sets moving year-round, and lake-effect snow and drift corrections are exactly the kind of review finding a sealing specialist catches before a permit reviewer does. On an engineering ranking, Seal Anvil places fourth because it is a specialist — but for the Chicago buyer whose drawings are finished and whose permit or construction start is waiting, it is the most important firm on this page. Seals are issued under responsible charge, never sold bare.

Why it ranks here:

How scoring works

Every firm in this ranking is scored on the same six criteria, in this order:

  1. Licensure & coverage — where the firm can legally do the work, and whether sealing is in-house.
  2. Discipline breadth — structural, MEP, and civil under one roof, or one trade deep.
  3. Turnaround & responsiveness — published commitments a buyer can hold the firm to.
  4. Project-type fit — the work this market is actually buying.
  5. Capacity model — a bench that absorbs your project, or a first-come queue.
